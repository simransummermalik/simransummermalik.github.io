What We Are Building

I started CAIR's Quantum Machine Learning division to make QML more approachable through real projects, peer learning, and reproducible experiments. Our main goal is QML Torch: an open-source framework that brings quantum computing into familiar PyTorch-style workflows. The idea is to make a quantum layer feel closer to adding a normal PyTorch layer, without requiring students to rebuild device setup, encodings, ansatzes, batching, gradient flow, and framework integration every time.

Accessibility is only half of the goal. We also compare hybrid quantum-classical models with strong classical baselines and ask where quantum methods provide a real, measurable advantage. We do not assume that quantum is automatically better. We want to understand which tasks, representations, circuits, and model architectures make quantum components useful, then use those advantages intentionally.

My Role

As division lead, I have built the technical direction, onboarding material, activities, meeting notes, project examples, resource guides, and the larger roadmap for QML Torch. I lead students from classical machine learning foundations into quantum feature maps, kernels, hybrid layers, model comparison, and honest evaluation of possible quantum advantage.
