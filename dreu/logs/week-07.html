Week 07 Research Log

Dates

July 20, 2026 - July 26, 2026

Overview

This week felt like the point where the project started becoming something I could actually stand in front of people and explain. We finished our poster, and I am really excited to present it next week. At the same time, it has been hard to break everything down into a clean, simple story because there is so much work behind it now: circuit construction, QFT precision, noisy sampling, lattice post-processing, tests, reproducibility notes, negative results, and all of the experiments that connect those pieces together.

That has been one of the biggest challenges of the project so far. The more work we do, the harder it becomes to explain it in a way that is accurate but still readable. I do not want to make the work sound simpler than it is, but I also do not want the audience to get lost before they understand why the project matters. A lot of this week was about finding that balance.

Poster and Presentation Preparation

The main milestone this week was finishing the research poster. The poster forced me to decide what the center of the story should be. The project has grown in many directions, but for the presentation I wanted to emphasize the full quantum-to-classical pipeline: Regev-style circuit ideas, multidimensional QFT behavior, noisy finite sampling, lattice reduction, and the reproducibility work needed to make the experiments checkable.

Making the poster also showed me which parts of the project are still difficult to communicate. Some pieces are very technical, especially the transition from quantum samples to lattice relations and then to possible factor extraction. Other pieces are conceptually important but easy to underestimate, like testing, fixed configurations, and documentation. I want people to understand that the code is not just producing a pretty circuit diagram. It is trying to make a careful experimental claim about what happens when the ideal math is pushed into finite, noisy, reproducible computation.

IEEE QCE 2026 Acceptance

The biggest good news this week is that my work was accepted to IEEE QCE 2026. I am so excited to attend. A few of my DREU friends were accepted as well, which makes it feel even more special because we have all been working so hard in parallel and now get to share that moment together.

The reviews were not simple. I received two borderline reviews and one accept, so a lot of effort went into editing the work, clarifying the claims, and changing parts of the writing to make the contribution stronger after the feedback. That process was stressful, but it also made the work better. It pushed me to be more precise about what I had actually shown, what was still experimental, and why the project matters even when the results are careful instead of flashy.

I am proud of that. That feels like a real part of becoming a researcher.

Hand-Tracking Demo

I also continued working on the hand-tracking demo as a way to make the research feel more interactive and approachable. The demo is not meant to replace the technical work. It is a communication tool: a way to help people engage with computational ideas through motion and visual feedback instead of only equations, code, and plots.

The current demo is here:

Hand-tracking demo

Working on the demo reminded me that research communication is also a design problem. If I want people to care about the technical work, I need to give them a path into it. The poster, the beginner guide, the reproducibility notes, and the demo are all different attempts to build that path.

Reflection

This week was exciting, but it was also overwhelming in a very honest way. There is so much material now that I sometimes feel like I am carrying too many threads at once. But that also means the project has depth. It has experiments, failures, revisions, documentation, and presentation work. It has the parts that worked and the parts that forced me to rethink my assumptions.

I am learning that research is not just getting one result. It is learning how to shape a messy, complicated process into something another person can inspect and understand. That is hard, but it is also exactly the kind of work I wanted to be doing.

Next Steps

Next week, the main goal is to present the poster clearly and confidently. I want to explain the work in a way that is honest about the complexity but still gives people a strong picture of what we built and why it matters.

I also want to keep working on the constant pieces of the project: improving tests, tightening the reproducibility guide, refining the experiments, applying stronger noise models, and making the hand-tracking demo more polished. The presentation is a milestone, but the work is still moving.
