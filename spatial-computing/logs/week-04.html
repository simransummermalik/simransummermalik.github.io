Week 04 Spatial Computing Research Log

Dates

March 23, 2026 - March 29, 2026

Persistent Spatial Representations

This week focused on how a live immersive application can maintain a model that is stable enough for algorithmic reasoning while still responding to new scene geometry. Raw mesh updates alone are too volatile. The system needs an intermediate layer that preserves spatial state across local changes.

I began developing a layered representation strategy: a lower-level geometric layer would preserve reconstruction data, while a higher-level graph or occupancy structure would expose decision-relevant information. The higher-level model could then be revised selectively instead of rebuilt after every incoming update.

Search in Partially Known Space

The offline graph had been generated from a complete dataset. A live model, however, is incomplete and uncertain while scanning continues. I started treating BFS, Dijkstra, and A* as algorithms operating over the system's current belief about space rather than over a final map.

Evaluation Criteria

  • Stability under new geometric updates
  • Efficiency of local or incremental revision
  • Interpretability of paths and reachability decisions
  • Fidelity to constraints that matter for navigation
  • Ability to support provisional decisions and later re-planning

Runtime Architecture

The visionOS application became part of the methodology rather than only a viewer. I used the Xcode prototype to reason about where spatial state should live, how updates should propagate, and what changes should trigger a new algorithmic query.
