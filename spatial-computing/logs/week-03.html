Week 03 Spatial Computing Research Log

Dates

March 16, 2026 - March 22, 2026

From Path Output to Explanation

I expanded the project beyond reporting whether an algorithm found a path. The new goal was to explain why a route emerged and which assumptions made that route possible. A graph is not a neutral copy of the room: it defines what counts as adjacency, continuity, and navigable space.

Downsampling from 681,270 raw points to 9,842 points, restricting the graph to 1,200 nodes, and choosing a distance threshold that produced 6,437 edges all changed what the algorithms could know. These were not only performance shortcuts. They were representational decisions with direct consequences for reachability and pathfinding.

Unified Pipeline

I consolidated the work into an end-to-end pipeline:

physical or modeled environment -> spatial representation -> graph abstraction -> algorithmic reasoning -> interpretable metrics

Shift Toward Live Spatial Computing

On March 21 and 22, I shifted attention from offline scans to live spatial acquisition. Instead of treating a scan as a finished dataset, I began asking how a Vision Pro application could continuously update an internal environment model while the user moved through space.

ARKit scene reconstruction now became a stream of partial geometric observations rather than a one-time result. This introduced new questions: when is a model stable enough for graph construction, how often should new observations be incorporated, and when should an algorithm re-plan?

Result

The research now connected the controlled offline experiments to a live immersive system in which representation, sensing, and decision-making evolve together.
