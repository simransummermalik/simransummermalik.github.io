Week 07 Spatial Computing Research Log

Dates

April 13, 2026 - April 19, 2026

Persistence Under Local Change

I focused on how much of the internal model should survive when new spatial evidence arrives. The system needs to absorb real changes without discarding every previous conclusion. This introduced questions about spatial memory, revision boundaries, and the difference between a local update and a change large enough to invalidate the model.

Stability Versus Adaptability

The representation must remain flexible enough to incorporate new geometry, but stable enough that algorithmic outputs remain interpretable. I began using this tension as a central evaluation lens for both the data structure and the search behavior.

Event-Driven Re-Planning

Re-planning after every small reconstruction update would be expensive and unstable. Waiting too long could leave the system using outdated assumptions. I therefore began framing re-computation as an event-driven process triggered by meaningful changes in decision-relevant structure.

Decision-Relevant Structure

The purpose of the internal model is not to retain every geometric detail. It should preserve the parts of space that matter for reachability, path plausibility, local continuity, and route revision. This made the representation goal more precise.

Component Order

I finalized the intended sequence as retention -> stabilization -> abstraction -> algorithmic reasoning. Additional reading on navigation under uncertainty and incremental modeling supported this systems-level focus.
