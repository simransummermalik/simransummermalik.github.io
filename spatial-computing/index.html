Name: Summer (Simran) Malik

Institution: University of North Carolina at Charlotte

Faculty Mentor: Dr. Todd Dobbs

Research Area: Spatial computing, spatial representations, and algorithmic decision-making

Project

Algorithmic Behavior Under Different Spatial Representations in Spatial Computing

Spatial computing systems can capture detailed three-dimensional environments, but algorithms cannot reason directly over raw spatial data. The environment must first be converted into a computational representation such as a point cloud, mesh, graph, occupancy structure, or region-based model. My research examined how that representation choice changes what an algorithm can perceive, which paths it finds, how much of the environment it considers reachable, and when its decisions remain stable.

I used an Apple Vision Pro and a visionOS prototype built with Swift, SwiftUI, RealityKit, and ARKit alongside offline point-cloud experiments. The algorithmic work compared breadth-first search, Dijkstra's shortest path algorithm, and A* across simplified spatial models. The project later expanded from static graph analysis into the systems problem of maintaining a persistent, decision-relevant world model while live reconstruction data continues to change.

Research Pipeline

Physical or modeled environment -> spatial representation -> stabilized abstraction -> graph or occupancy structure -> algorithmic reasoning -> interpretable outputs
